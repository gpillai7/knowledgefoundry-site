KF-5000 · Exemplars & Case Studies

Worked instantiations of the architecture — illustrative, not normative

Series Note (Illustrative) · Draft · v0.1

Purpose

The KF-5000 series shows the Knowledge Foundry architecture at work on concrete Operational Knowledge Objects. Its documents teach and demonstrate; they do not legislate. Like the KF-0000 navigation series, KF-5000 introduces no new architectural concept — every element it shows is defined in its home document (Canon, Lifecycle, Materiality, and the KF-3000 series) and is only instantiated here.

The series exists because an architecture is easier to trust once it has been seen to run. A reader who has followed one governed object from capture to retirement understands the whole shape of the system before studying any single part in depth.

The distinction the series preserves

  • KF-5001 · Canonical Vertical Slice is illustrative: a domain-neutral object (a four-day-week Knowledge Brief) constructed to make every architectural stage visible. It proves the parts fit together and that the architecture is subject-independent. It does not validate the architecture — a constructed object cannot surprise its designer.
  • KF-5002+ · Case Studies are evaluative: real domain instantiations — beginning with MacroLab’s Singapore Business Impact Assessment — in which the architecture meets uncontrolled reality. These are where the architecture is stress-tested, and where the Draft for Experiment documents (KF-2003, KF-3007, KF-0002) are eventually validated before baselining.

The order is deliberate: understand the architecture on the canonical slice; then evaluate it on real cases. MacroLab is thereby positioned as a case study of Knowledge Foundry, not as its defining example.

Relationship to the rest of the programme

KF-5000 sits beside KF-4000 (Implementation): where KF-4000 defines how the architecture is built in a deployment, KF-5000 shows how it behaves on individual objects. Both are downstream of the architecture and neither may revise it. For where every concept is defined, see KF-3009 (Crosswalks) and KF-0002 (Controlled Vocabulary).
